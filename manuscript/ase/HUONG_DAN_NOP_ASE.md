### Abstract (dán vào ô Abstract)

Influence-area bridge weigh-in-motion divides the area under a response record by an integral constant. On continuous girders this constant can vanish, because influence lines change sign, and a gauge where it vanishes cannot weigh. The paper gives three main results. First, closed forms locate these blind points for five channel types: strain gauges measuring global bending are blind at every zero of the uniform-load moment and nearly blind 0.19–0.32 of the span from the interior support on two equal spans, whereas deflection gauges are blind only for particular span ratios. Second, for position-independent noise, the best sensor locations are the extremes of the uniform-load response; on one day of public records, weighting gauges by vehicle-free noise covariance raised the error 8.5 percentage points above the gauge average. Third, for symmetric girders with equal piers the constant is linear-fractional in pier stiffness; on an in-service box girder, two static readings leave the side-span deflection constant of the base model uncertain by a factor of 6.6 against 1.5 at mid main span (2.4 against 1.1 with girder rigidity bounded to 0.9–1.3 times the design value). On this girder, a single deflection gauge belongs at mid main span.
