**Submission of an original research paper: "Total-load observability of continuous girder bridges from a single deflection channel: closed-form blind configurations, laboratory verification and application to an in-service box-girder bridge"**

We submit the above manuscript for consideration as an original research paper in *Structures*.

Influence-area bridge weigh-in-motion estimates the gross weight of a vehicle from the area under a single response record. On continuous girders the influence line changes sign over the interior supports, and the positive and negative areas partly cancel. The manuscript asks a question that comes before any weighing: for a given continuous girder and sensor position, can one deflection channel observe the total load at all?

The paper makes the following contributions.

1. By reciprocity, the constant that relates the deflection integral to the total load is the deflection at the sensor under a uniformly distributed unit load. This makes the constant available from one static analysis or one static test, before installation. We present this as the foundation of the work and credit the influence-area idea to Ojio and Yamada and to the later strain-area formulations cited in the paper.
2. Closed forms of this constant for two-span beams with arbitrary span and stiffness ratios and for symmetric three-span beams, which locate the blind configurations exactly. For a three-span beam instrumented at mid side span with uniform rigidity, the blind main-to-side span ratio is 1.700594.
3. Two pre-installation indicators: the normalised constant, and the cancellation ratio $C_m/A^+$, which does not depend on a reference stiffness and is better suited to haunched girders.
4. An error budget for the integral estimator, which shows that zero-offset drift rather than random noise dominates in the field.

The scope of the evidence is stated plainly in the manuscript. The theory is verified against finite element evaluations and on a laboratory two-span steel beam with statically positioned loads, where two- and three-load groups were identified within 1.19 % and 0.70 % of direct weighing. The criterion is then applied to the acceptance-test data of an in-service 55 + 90 + 55 m prestressed concrete box girder. That application shows that the installed side-span gauge is near-blind, but it is a static consistency check: no crossing record exists for the bridge, so the integral identity itself is not validated on a continuous girder. Moving-truck records from two simply supported bridges are used only to examine baseline drift and filtering, and their limitations are listed.

We believe the paper suits *Structures* because it gives structural engineers a closed-form, design-stage check for placing a deflection sensor on continuous girders, supported by laboratory verification and by data from an in-service bridge.
