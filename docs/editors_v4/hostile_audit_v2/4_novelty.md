# Hostile audit v2, role 4: novelty (BWIM, influence lines, sensor placement, identifiability)

Scope: Introduction contributions (i)-(iii), abstract, Sections 2.2, 2.4, 2.5, 5.3 and the cover letter, checked against the literature. Bibliographic data below were checked against Crossref records (api.crossref.org). Where I could read only an abstract or a search-engine excerpt, I say so. Paywalled full texts (Elsevier, ASCE) could not be opened, Scholar Gateway was over its quota, and Firecrawl had no credits, so this is a targeted search, not a systematic review.

Overall: I found no published work that already gives (a) a catalog of blind or near-blind loci for influence-area weighing on continuous girders, (b) an extreme-point placement rule for gross-weight (influence-area) BWIM, or (c) a Möbius-law identifiability analysis of the influence-area constant under pier-stiffness uncertainty. The three headline claims survive. But each one leans on classical or recent results that the paper does not cite, and two sentences overstate the gap.

## Blocking

None found. No verified prior publication pre-empts contributions (i)-(iii), and the novelty statements I checked contain no wrong citations.

## Should-fix

**S1. Missing key prior work: the distribution of strain-record area along continuous girders (Introduction para. 3, the gap sentence; contribution (i); cover letter item 1).**
Quote: "yet none of these studies gives closed-form conditions under which this assumption fails". Also (i): "its strain criterion, the zeros of the uniform-load moment, holds for any girder in beam theory".
Problem: an analytical description of how the area of a strain history varies along a continuous girder has already been published, for damage detection. Wu B, Wu G, Yang C and He Y (2018) Damage identification method for continuous girder bridges based on spatially-distributed long-gauge strain sensing under moving loads. *Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing* 104: 415–435. DOI: 10.1016/j.ymssp.2017.10.040 (Crossref verified). According to the abstract, as returned in search excerpts (full text not read), it derives "a calculation method for the distribution of the area under the long-gauge strain history". That area distribution is parabolic when the vehicle crosses the monitored span and linear when it crosses other spans, and the method "is not affected by the speed, axle number and weight of vehicles". The sum of those span contributions is exactly the uniform-load moment diagram of Eq. (3)/(7). The strain blind points of Table 1 are therefore the zeros of an area distribution already derived for continuous girders, even though Wu et al. do not read those zeros as blind points for weighing. A related paper, Hong W, Cao Y and Wu Z (2016) Strain-based damage-assessment method for bridges under moving vehicular loads using long-gauge strain sensing. *Journal of Bridge Engineering* 21(10): 04016059. DOI: 10.1061/(ASCE)BE.1943-5592.0000933 (Crossref verified), uses the strain time-history area ratio (STHAR). Per the publisher abstract excerpt, STHAR depends only on local bending stiffness and is independent of vehicle weight, speed and axle number. That is the same identity used in ratio form.
Fix: cite Wu et al. (2018) and Hong et al. (2016) in Introduction para. 3 and next to Eq. (3). Narrow the gap sentence to something like: "the distribution of strain-record area along continuous girders has been derived for damage detection (Hong et al., 2016; Wu et al., 2018), but no study reads its zeros as blind loci for weighing or gives the corresponding conditions for other channels and for uncertain boundary conditions".

**S2. The "Möbius law" is presented as a contribution, but its Sherman–Morrison basis and the classical rank-one/fixity-factor literature are uncited (abstract; contribution (iii); Section 2.5, Eq. (13); Conclusion 3; cover letter item 3).**
Quotes: "the constant depends on pier stiffness through a linear-fractional (Möbius) law"; "the Sherman–Morrison formula gives (Supplementary Section S5)"; "Equation (13) is called the Möbius law."
Problem: for any linear structure whose stiffness depends on a single spring through a rank-one term, every static response is a linear-fractional function of that spring stiffness. This is the Sherman–Morrison result and is standard in exact structural reanalysis. Fixity-factor formulations of semi-rigid connections write member stiffness through the factor 1/(1 + c·EI/(kL)) for the same reason. The paper uses Sherman–Morrison but cites no source for it. Naming the result a "law" and listing it as a contribution, without this context, overstates its novelty. What is new is applying it to the influence-area constant, the symmetry argument that reduces two piers to one pattern, and the Mây Bridge numbers.
Verified references to add: Sherman J and Morrison WJ (1950) Adjustment of an inverse matrix corresponding to a change in one element of a given matrix. *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 21(1): 124–127. DOI: 10.1214/aoms/1177729893. Akgün MA, Garcelon JH and Haftka RT (2001) Fast exact linear and non-linear structural reanalysis and the Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury formulas. *International Journal for Numerical Methods in Engineering* 50(7): 1587–1606. DOI: 10.1002/nme.87. Monforton GR and Wu TS (1963) Matrix analysis of semi-rigidly connected frames. *Journal of the Structural Division (ASCE)* 89(6): 13–42. DOI: 10.1061/JSDEAG.0000997.
Fix: cite these in Section 2.5. In (iii), say "follows the linear-fractional form that a rank-one boundary term implies (Sherman and Morrison, 1950; Akgün et al., 2001)", and drop or soften "is called the Möbius law".

**S3. Contribution (ii) is framed against a non-comparable reference, and the extreme-point rule is textbook (Introduction para. 4 (ii); Section 2.4 para. 2; cover letter item 2).**
Quote: "Extreme-point optimal design (Elfving, 1952) gives a closed-form placement rule in place of numerical optimization (Zhou et al., 2025)".
Problem: Zhou C, Demartino C, Narazaki Y, Xu Y, Chatzi E and Spencer BF (2025), *MSSP* 241: 113522, DOI 10.1016/j.ymssp.2025.113522 (Crossref verified), optimizes sensors for time-domain axle and moving-force estimation with an augmented Kalman filter. That is a different estimand and a different model, so "in place of" implies that the closed form replaces their optimization, which it does not. The rule itself is the classical optimal-design result. For a single parameter, the c-optimal design is supported where |C| is largest. With a nuisance common offset (an intercept), the optimal slope design puts points at the two ends of the design range. Elfving covers the first case. The second is standard design theory, e.g., Pukelsheim F (2006) *Optimal Design of Experiments* (Classics in Applied Mathematics 50). Philadelphia: SIAM. DOI: 10.1137/1.9780898719109.
Fix: reword (ii) as "classical extreme-point optimal design (Elfving, 1952; Pukelsheim, 2006) applied to the influence-area constant gives a closed-form rule for gross-weight channels; numerical placement has been posed for axle-load estimation (Zhou et al., 2025)". Present the zero-sum case as the classical two-endpoint design for a slope with a nuisance offset.

**S4. Missing continuous-bridge BWIM paper in the target journal (Introduction para. 3; cover letter para. "The manuscript builds on bridge weigh-in-motion work published in this journal").**
Problem: Yamaguchi E, Kawamura S, Matuso K, Matsuki Y and Naito Y (2009) Bridge-weigh-in-motion by two-span continuous bridge with skew and heavy-truck flow in Fukuoka area, Japan. *Advances in Structural Engineering* 12(1): 115–125. DOI: 10.1260/136943309787522614 (Crossref verified, abstract read). It is an ASE paper on BWIM over a two-span continuous bridge: the bridge was "far from ideal for the weigh-in-motion", a preliminary test was run to check feasibility, and the maximum error was 11.7 % over 237 days of operation. This is exactly the setting of Table 1's two-span results, and it appeared in the journal that the cover letter says the manuscript builds on. (From the abstract I cannot tell whether it used the influence-area or the Moses method.)
Fix: cite it in Introduction para. 3 among continuous-bridge weighing studies, and add it to the cover-letter list of ASE papers.

**S5. The cover letter overstates the tiltmeter finding (cover letter item 3).**
Quote: "and a pier tiltmeter is the most informative additional measurement."
Problem: Section 5.3 says "the most informative of the measurements compared", evaluated "at the design pier stiffness". The comparison covers the two deflections, an end-bearing rotation and conditional frequencies (Supplementary S8). The integral ratio, which Section 6 lists as an alternative, was not assessed with the Cramér–Rao bound. The unqualified cover-letter claim goes beyond the evidence.
Fix: "and, of the measurements compared, a pier tiltmeter is the most informative at the design pier stiffness."

## Minor

**M1. The strain blind points are textbook inflection points (Section 2.2, Eq. (7); Table 1, strain row; Conclusion 1).** ξ0 = 1 − μ/4 is 0.75 for two equal spans, the contraflexure point listed in standard beam tables (R1 = 3qL/8, so M = 0 at x = 3L/4). Ghali et al. (2017) is cited for μ, which is fair. Still, the contribution sentence should make clear that the novelty is reading these zeros, and the near-blind band around them, as blind loci for weighing, not the zeros themselves. The text partly does this already ("Neither the influence-area identity ... is new").

**M2. Area-based influence-line features with sensor positioning are uncited (Section 2.3, the cancellation ratio R).** Döring A, Vogelbacher M, Schneider O, Müller J, Hinz S and Matthes J (2023) Investigations of ratio-based integrated influence lines as features for bridge-damage detection. *Infrastructures* 8(4): 72. DOI: 10.3390/infrastructures8040072 (abstract read). It integrates strain and deflection influence lines analytically (Euler–Bernoulli) and considers sensor positioning. An earlier version of this manuscript cited it; the current one does not. Huang D and Lu Y (2025) Influence line theory supported single sensor-based strain feature for bridge defect diagnosis. *Structural Health Monitoring* (online), DOI: 10.1177/14759217251376931, uses single-sensor influence-line ratio features on a two-span continuous bridge. Fix: cite Döring et al. (2023) next to R (Eq. (9)).

**M3. The sensor-placement literature is thin (Introduction para. 3: "Sensor placement for BWIM has been posed through an augmented Kalman filter (Zhou et al., 2025)").** D-optimal placement for moving-load identification exists: Augustine et al. (2015) Dynamic moving load identification using optimal sensor placement. Proc. ASME IMECE 2015, Vol. 13. DOI: 10.1115/IMECE2015-50701 (Crossref title/authors only). D-optimal strain-gauge placement that cancels nuisance loads, the analogue of the zero-sum estimator, also exists: Iriarte X, Bacaicoa J, Aginaga J, Plaza A and Szczepańska-Álvarez A (2025) D-optimal strain sensor placement for mechanical load estimation in the presence of nuisance loads and thermal strain. *Sensors and Actuators A: Physical* 382: 116110. DOI: 10.1016/j.sna.2024.116110 (Crossref). Optional citations.

DOI: 10.1088/1361-665X/ace4ac (abstract read: "the envelope area of the long-gauge strain influence line is directly proportional to the vehicle weight"). Optional, next to Yang et al. (2021).

**M5. Recent review.** Cai Q, Chen Z and Zhu S (2025) A comprehensive review of bridge influence lines: from identifications to applications in bridge health monitoring. *Structural Health Monitoring* (online). DOI: 10.1177/14759217251350415. A single citation in Introduction para. 3 would show the authors know the current state of influence-line research.

**M6. α–k non-identifiability from static tests has engineering precedent (Section 2.5 para. 1; Section 5.2).** "Every static response therefore has the form f(κ)/α ... Only the ratio of two static readings ... informs κ." That load tests confound girder rigidity with unintended support restraint is well documented: Bakht B and Jaeger LG (1988) Bearing restraint in slab-on-girder bridges. *Journal of Structural Engineering* 114(12): 2724–2740, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(1988)114:12(2724). Bakht B and Jaeger LG (1990) Bridge testing—a surprise every time. *Journal of Structural Engineering* 116(5): 1370–1383, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(1990)116:5(1370). Both Crossref verified. Citing one of them would ground the "effective parameters" argument of Section 5.1.
