## X

Strengths: (1) a clear narrative arc (B-WIM background, then performance indicators, then the influence-line method, then monitoring) that a specialist follows easily; (2) the intro and the opening paragraph of each section say what is coming; (3) the bridges and the rotational-stiffness coefficient are explained in plain, concrete terms, and the bullet list of conclusions after Table 2 helps. Problems: (1) frequent non-native slips ("also know as", "which boundary conditions changed", "increase for 7 and 11%", "calculating of", "a further comprehension", "the respected temperature range"), plus list artefacts ("1. 1."); (2) notation is loose around Eq. (2): the indices j, n and i are mixed, K and N swap roles, and Eq. (4) and (5) are separated from their "where" clauses by figure and table callouts; (3) the long Sect. 2 history and the GDF/DAF detour dilute the focus, and the conclusions name Bridge C for a figure the text attributes to Bridges C and D.

## Y

Strengths: (1) excellent signposting: each section opens with its question, the contributions are numbered, and a final workflow pulls everything together; (2) crisp, precise sentences that state limits honestly ("These are predictions, not results"); (3) equations are introduced and interpreted in prose, and the tables carry CIs, clear captions and footnotes. Problems: (1) very high density: a 60-row nomenclature and many indicators, ratios and constants (N_w, N_M, R, B_m, kappa, r_87, P_delta) put a heavy load on working memory; (2) the abstract is packed with undefined or lightly defined terms and numbers, so it is hard to read cold; (3) Sections 4 to 6 lean heavily on supplementary material and cross-references, and the many caveats (margins, CIs, conditional interpretations) sometimes obscure the main message.

## Z

Strengths: (1) the core idea (use the absolute area to avoid cancellation) is simple and stated early; (2) the case tables (Tables 2 to 5, 7) make the simulation set-up easy to scan; (3) the numbered conclusions are short. Problems: (1) the structure is broken: Sections 3 and 4 have no top-level headings, a sentence is duplicated in Section 2 ("Equation (4) can be named..." appears twice, before Eq. (4) is defined), and figures are cited out of order; (2) there are many grammar and wording errors ("will not be illness", "Cina", "first and third modals", "sensor's line of influence", "inverse problem of the dynamic") and vague claims ("outstanding", "eliminate the effect of noise"); (3) the introduction is a citation list without a clear gap, and the notation and numbers are inconsistent (bridge length 360 m vs 540 m, "Chengxi" vs "Chenxi", lambda and phi defined loosely, Table 4 "0.5 mean" vs "uniform").

## Ranking

1. **Y (6.7)**: the clearest structure and most precise prose, held back mainly by its very heavy notation and density.
2. **X (6.2)**: easy to follow as a story, but grammar slips and loose notation create steady friction.
3. **Z (3.6)**: broken structure, duplicated text and frequent language errors make it hard to read even for a specialist.
