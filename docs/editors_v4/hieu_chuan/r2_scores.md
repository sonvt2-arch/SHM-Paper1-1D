## X: blind loci and design criteria for integral load-monitoring channels

**Strengths.** (1) The structure and signposting are excellent. Every section opens by saying what it asks, the field section closes with "what the records show", and Section 7 turns the results into a numbered workflow. (2) Sentences are short, declarative and precise. Claims are hedged explicitly ("These are predictions, not results"; "a coarse check ..., not a sharp test"), so the reader always knows what has been shown. (3) Equations are introduced in the prose, symbols are defined where they are used, and each closed form is followed by a sentence that interprets it (for example "A deflection blind point therefore exists only for 1<μ<2").

**Problems.** (1) The information density is very high. Almost every paragraph carries several numbers, CIs or ranges, and the reader must hold many quantities at once (κ, α, k_g, B_m, R, N_w, N_M, τ, r_87, calibrations A/B). (2) The notation load is heavy: a 60-row nomenclature, two indicator normalisations with different thresholds (2.5 versus 1), and several near-synonyms (constant, indicator, cancellation ratio). The text defines all of them carefully, but they still cause friction. (3) The abstract is long and reads as a list of findings with little framing. It mixes theory, laboratory, field and in-service results, and the "factor of 6.6" result needs context the abstract does not give. Section 5 is also long and dense.

## Y: absolute-area influence-line method for continuous rigid-frame bridges

**Strengths.** (1) The core idea (integrate the absolute displacement record instead of the signed one) is simple and can be grasped quickly. (2) The simulation cases are laid out in small tables (Tables 2-5), and Table 5 gives one compact comparison of the three methods. (3) The conclusions are numbered and short, and each one maps roughly onto a result section.

**Problems.** (1) The structure is broken. Section 3 and its 3.1 heading are missing (the text jumps from Section 2 to 3.1.1), Section 4 has no heading, and in Section 2 one sentence ("In this case, Equation (4) can be named...") appears twice, almost word for word. (2) Sentence-level clarity is poor. There are many errors and awkward constructions ("will not be illness", "Cina", "Chengxi"/"Chenxi", "first and third modals", "His method", "accuracy is proportional to the data range"). The abstract and introduction lean on generic boilerplate ("novel", "outstanding", "innovative algorithms"). (3) The notation and numbers are inconsistent, which makes the results hard to follow. The bridge length is given as 360 m and as 540 m. The noise is called white, uniform and harmonic in different places. Table 4 is titled "0.5 mean noise" but describes the noise as "uniform random". Equation (4) is attributed before it appears. "IL" names both the concept and a method. The introduction is a citation list with weak links between items and no clear gap statement.

## Z: using bridge weigh-in-motion systems to monitor single-span bridge influence lines

**Strengths.** (1) The paper has a clear narrative arc (B-WIM basics, performance indicators, influence-line calculation, robustness, temperature, before/after rehabilitation). Section openers and the introduction's roadmap guide the reader. (2) The information density is comfortable for practitioners, and the concepts (GDF, DAF, the rotational-stiffness coefficient F) are explained in plain language with physical interpretation. (3) The results on the rehabilitated bridge are summarised as bulleted conclusions with absolute and relative changes, and the conclusions candidly state what remains open.

**Problems.** (1) There are recurring grammar and idiom errors ("also know as", "which boundary conditions changed", "increase for 7 and 11%", "influenced lines", "A further comprehension of the study"). (2) Notation is applied loosely in places. In Eq. (2) the description swaps N and K and A_i/A_n, F is used for a coefficient that is described in several ways, and the temperature effect is stated as an increase in Section 4.2 and as a decrease in the conclusions. (3) The results tables (Tables 1 and 2) arrive without content in this version. The text leans on figures, and the long background on GDF/DAF dilutes the stated focus on influence lines. Some passages are digressive (for example the history of B-WIM, and the influence-surface discussion inside the results).

## Ranking by overall readability

1. **X (7.2).** It is the most carefully edited and best signposted, with precise, hedged prose. Its very high density and notation load keep it below the "frictionless" 8.
2. **Z (6.6).** It is clear and easy to follow at the narrative level, but grammar slips, loose notation and thin results presentation cause recurring friction.
3. **Y (3.9).** Missing section headings, a duplicated passage, frequent language errors and inconsistent numbers and noise definitions make it hard to read even when the method is simple.
